# Procurement Intelligence Report: AI Production Equipment — US Federal & Corporate Channels, H1 2026

## 1. Channel overview & method

**Platforms searched (all via websearch; samples verified via webfetch):**
- **SAM.gov** (opportunities + awards) — surfaced through search-engine-indexed copies of `sam.gov/workspace/contract/opp/…` records and aggregators
- **Aggregators/caches:** HigherGov, Cleatus, SweetSpotGov, GovCB, Starbridge, GovContractFinder, PrimeRFP (daily award digests), sbir.gov / sbir.org (SBIR award database)
- **Agency primary sites:** army.mil, DVIDS, war.gov, darpa.mil, navysbir.com
- **Corporate/trade press:** WardsAuto, AssemblyMag, The Robot Report, Manufacturing Digital, metrology.news, The Elec, Asia Business Daily

**Method:** ~12 keyword searches (8 broad procurement searches + 4 corporate/verification searches) and 7 direct `webfetch` verifications.

**Critical access finding:** Direct `webfetch` of SAM.gov returns only a JavaScript shell ("Sam Acquisition 360") — the site is a client-rendered SPA and cannot be crawled without an account/API. Public SAM.gov content is only reachable via (a) search-engine indexing of individual records, (b) paid/free aggregators that mirror the data, or (c) GSA's SAM.gov API/FPDS bulk feeds (requires registration). This materially constrains the channel.

**Verification status legend:**
- 【VERIFIED】 = record opened and content confirmed directly via webfetch of a primary source or aggregator page
- (indexed) = record content confirmed only via search-engine-indexed snippet/copy of sam.gov or aggregator result (direct fetch blocked: JS shell, 403, or transport error)
- (primary) = agency primary-source URL (army.mil, war.gov, darpa.mil) confirmed via search snippet; direct fetch blocked by transport error

**Verified roster:** F1 【VERIFIED】 (HigherGov + DVIDS), F2 【VERIFIED】 (HigherGov), C1 【VERIFIED】 (WardsAuto). All other rows are (indexed) or (primary).

## 4. Signal density assessment

- **Monthly volume estimate (US federal, public-visible):** H1 2026 searches surfaced ~10–15 distinct qualifying US federal signals (production + R&D) in ~6 weeks of searching effort — roughly **2–4 per month** visible via open search/aggregators. This is a *lower bound*: SAM.gov contains many more generic-titled records (e.g., "Robotic Arms," "Cobot," "Automated Scanning System," "Vision System") whose AI content lives in the SOW attachment, so keyword recall on the public web misses a large share. A paid FPDS/SAM API crawl would likely multiply the count 3–5×, but requires an account (free registration) or commercial aggregator.
- **Data quality:**
  - *Award amounts:* disclosed for ~5 of 18 federal samples (F1 $39.2M/$80M, F2 $5M, F8 $635M, F9 $21M, F10 $36M, F18 $1.8M) — roughly 50–60% when separating awards from solicitations/RFIs. **RFIs/sources-sought carry no amount** by design (~40% of pipeline).
  - *AI features:* well-documented in SOW text but inconsistently placed (title vs. description vs. attachment). Machine-learning/computer-vision language appears in most sampled records.
  - *Labor/output metrics:* **the weakest field.** Explicit throughput/manpower evidence ("orders-of-magnitude," "reduce manpower," "12,000 rounds/mo") appears in only ~30–40% of federal procurement records; it is far more common in agency press releases (army.mil/DVIDS) and SBIR award narratives than in the contract notices themselves. The richest labor/output data is in the **corporate** channel (Ford: 150M inspections, 400K flags, 40 systems in 2026; Boeing: 17 hrs/aircraft) — but corporate procurement is opaque and press-sourced.
- **Operability score: 3/5.** The channel is usable as a qualitative/thematic leading indicator without an account, but not cleanly quantifiable: SAM.gov's SPA blocks headless crawling (no pagination/data export without login), keyword sensitivity is poor (generic titles), amount disclosure is partial, and the production-vs-R&D split requires manual reading of every record. With an FPDS-API crawl + a manual classification pass, this could rise to 4/5. Corporate side is 2/5 operability (no central registry; must triangulate trade press and supplier announcements).

## 5. Feasibility conclusion (≥300 words)

US federal procurement is a **usable but imperfect leading indicator** for "does AI create new physical wealth," and the verdict depends on which half of the question you are asking. For *verifying that AI production equipment is being bought by real manufacturing entities,* the signal is strong and getting stronger. H1 2026 shows a clear, accelerating pattern concentrated in the defense Organic Industrial Base: Tobyhanna Army Depot leasing robotic wire-harness production machines ($5M), Red River Army Depot standing up a $39.2M/$80M AI-automated machining facility run by Hadrian, the $635M robotics-and-SCADA Future Artillery Complex breaking ground at Iowa AAP, plus a $21M AMTEC 40mm line and a $36M Parsons LAP expansion, all within months of each other. These are not lab purchases — they are factory-floor equipment acquisitions by depots and government-owned ammunition plants, which is exactly the "physical wealth" signal the observatory wants. The accompanying policy scaffolding (FY26 NDAA provision directing the Army to expand robotic LAP automation, AMC's new part-qualification authority, AdvMARTDC's $100M AI-manufacturing IDIQ, DARPA Carbon Crunch's throughput-metric program) reinforces that this is a deliberate, funded shift rather than noise.

The weaknesses are structural. First, **the buyer base is narrow and policy-driven**: the strongest federal signals come from munitions/artillery surge production and depot modernization, so the federal channel is a *defense-specific* sub-indicator, not a proxy for US industrial AI adoption broadly. R&D contamination is severe — DARPA, NIST, and SBIR awards (Carbon Crunch, CHAMP, EnergeticAI, NIST workcells, Navy AI/ML-for-AM) are abundant and superficially resemble production buys, and a naive crawler would over-count them badly; they must be tagged and excluded. Second, **the metadata is shallow**: solicitation titles are generic ("Cobot"), amounts are absent from ~40% of notices (all RFIs), and explicit labor/output/throughput metrics — the very evidence the observatory needs — appear in only a minority of procurement records and must be manually enriched from agency press releases. Third, **the data cannot be reliably auto-collected**: SAM.gov is a JavaScript SPA that defeated direct fetch and will defeat naive crawlers; usable volume requires an account/API or a paid aggregator subscription. Operability is therefore ~3/5 as-is, ~4/5 with an FPDS-API feed plus a manual classification pass.

The corporate channel is analytically richer but operationally useless as a regular feed: no central registry exists, so one must assemble press releases and supplier announcements (Ford/IBM, Boeing, GM, BMW/Figure, Deere, Doosan). It confirms direction (hundreds of installations, 17 plants, 40 systems/2026, 150M inspections) but cannot be time-indexed or counted reliably.

**Bottom line:** Feasible as a *monthly qualitative leading-indicator feed* — with the specific sub-goal of tracking defense-industrial AI production capacity — provided the observatory (a) uses a paid FPDS/SAM-API pull, (b) applies a strict production-vs-R&D classifier (excluding DARPA/SBIR/NIST and depot-adjacent labs), and (c) augments each award with agency-press labor/throughput evidence. It should **not** be used alone as a quantitative index of US manufacturing AI adoption, because the federal channel reflects munitions-surge policy more than commercial economics; pair it with the corporate/trade-press channel for the true "new physical wealth" read.
